Mission 1The AI Spotter

Max says: "Not everything a computer does is AI! A system counts as AI when it does something we would call intelligent, usually by learning patterns from data instead of following one fixed rule. Can you tell them apart?"
How to play: For each card, predict AI or Not AI, then click. Read the feedback: it explains why. Guide link: Study Guide Chapter 1.

Mission 2The Pass-or-Fail Machine

Max says: "I must decide which fruit is an apple. I can only read colour and shape. Smell is unreadable data for me. Build my rule, then walk me through all five steps."
Steps: (1) Input: look at the training fruit. (2) Processing: tick the colours and shapes your rule accepts. (3) Outcomes: press Run rule to get pass/fail. (4) Adjustments: change the ticks to fix the fails. (5) Assessments: press Test on new fruit to see if your rule works on fruit it has never seen.

Your rule: "It's an apple if…"

Mission 3Name That AI

Max says: "People make big claims about AI. Classify each system by capability: Narrow, General (AGI) or Super. Remember which ones actually exist today!"
How to play: Choose a type for each card. Guide link: Study Guide Chapter 3.

Mission 4The Smart Warehouse

Max says: "A warehouse wants to hire some Narrow AI. Which technology fits each job: computer vision, robotics or an expert system?"
How to play: Pick the best fit. Some jobs need two technologies working together; the feedback tells you. Guide link: Study Guide Chapter 4.

Mission 5Choose Your Tool

Max says: "AI is not always the right answer! For each requirement, choose traditional programming or AI."
How to play: Decide, then read which trade-off settled it (control, data, learning, stability, scalability or 24/7 use). Guide link: Study Guide Chapter 5.

Mission 6The Family Detective (real Prolog)

Max says: "Symbolic AI knows only what you write down. This is a real Prolog engine running in your browser. Write facts and rules, predict the answer, then ask a question."
Syntax in 30 seconds: parent(tom, alice). is a fact. Lowercase words are things; Uppercase words like X are variables Prolog fills in. :- means "is true if", a comma means "and". Every line ends with a full stop. Guide link: Study Guide Activity 6A.
