### 8.1 Behaviour-based AI

By the 1980s, researchers found it hard to use symbolic AI in real robots. This led to a "reactive" approach: low-level behaviours that respond to the world directly, without building a detailed internal model first. Rodney Brooks introduced the **subsumption architecture** for this (arXiv:1611.05379).

- A subsumption architecture is a **hierarchy of simple behaviours** (Wooldridge, Parsons and Payne, course slides).
- Each behaviour is a rather simple, rule-like structure, and complex behaviour **emerges** from simple components interacting with the environment (same slides).
- Higher layers can take over (subsume) lower ones when appropriate (University of York Brooks page).

Brooks later founded iRobot, the company behind the Roomba robot vacuum, but the subsumption approach has been criticised as hard to scale to more complex robots (arXiv:1611.05379).

**Misconception check:** "Behaviour-based means no rules." Not quite. The behaviours are simple rules; the difference is that there is no large symbolic model of the world.
